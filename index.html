About Karen

Research shaped by healthcare leadership, education, and practice

Karen M. Blackwell is a marketing researcher, university educator, and former healthcare executive whose work examines consumer behavior in consequential service encounters. As a doctoral researcher in marketing at Virginia Tech’s Pamplin College of Business, she studies how people interpret expertise, navigate institutional interactions, and make decisions in settings with meaningful personal or societal consequences.

Her interdisciplinary research interests span healthcare and patient experience, expert guidance, information interpretation, measurement development, and marketing and public policy. Her scholarship is informed by executive leadership experience in healthcare marketing, strategy, market access, commercialization, and organizational growth.

Karen has also founded and led a research-informed marketing practice, translating consumer insight, data, and strategy into practical organizational decisions. Across multiple California State University campuses, she connects marketing theory with applied decision-making, inclusive course design, and students’ professional development.

Flagship research program
The problem

Broad patient-experience measures may not isolate specific encounters in which patients feel their concerns were not given appropriate consideration.

The research focus

Develop a clearly bounded, encounter-level approach to studying patient-perceived medical dismissal.

The potential contribution

Advance research in consumer behavior, healthcare quality, patient experience, and public policy.

Current stage: Construct and measurement development

Current flagship research

Medical dismissal

Developing a clearer way to study patient-perceived healthcare invalidation at the level of a specific encounter.

The project examines how these experiences may shape later health-information interpretation and responses to expert guidance. Public materials communicate the research problem, broad aims, and potential relevance while protecting unpublished items, models, hypotheses, and detailed study procedures.

  • Encounter-level construct development
  • Qualitative research and applied-measurement preparation
  • Interdisciplinary relevance for healthcare and public policy
